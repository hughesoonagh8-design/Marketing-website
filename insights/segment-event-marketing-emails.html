How do you get under the skin of each event audience segment?

By Oonagh Hughes · 3 November 2026 · 4 min read

Spend time in their world before you write a word. Talk to the people in your business who own each vertical, and dig into last year’s show data: which sessions each group went to, what they said in feedback, and who actually turned up. AI can help you write faster, but it can’t tell you what a hotel general manager is worried about this year. On the conference I ran for four years, working this way grew email-driven registrations by 148%.

Splitting the list is the easy part

Anyone can split a list by job title or sector. The hard part is knowing what to say once you have. A “hospitality” email full of generic copy is still a generic email, just sent to fewer people.

The difference comes from understanding each segment well enough to write the first line they’d actually want to read.

Talk to the people who own each vertical

Sales leads, account managers and vertical owners speak to these audiences every week. They know things no dashboard will tell you. For Cvent CONNECT Europe, I spent time with the people who owned each vertical before writing for it. Questions worth asking:

  • What is this group under pressure about right now?
  • What do they ask about on calls?
  • What gets them to say yes to a meeting, or to an event?
  • What words do they use for their own problems?

Write the answers down in their words. They’re nearly always better than anything you’d come up with at your desk.

Dig into last year’s show data

Registration data tells you who signed up. Show data tells you what they actually came for.

  • Session attendance by segment: which sessions each group chose once they were in the room.
  • Session feedback: what they rated highly, and the language they used to describe it.
  • Show rate by segment and ticket type: who turns up, and who doesn’t. (Free passes are usually the culprit: here’s why free passes hurt your show rate.)
  • Email results by segment: what each group opened, clicked and registered from last time.

This is where the specifics come from. If a segment packed out one type of session last year, lead with that. If they rated a networking lunch highly, name it in the first line.

Where AI fits

AI earned its place on CONNECT Europe. I built an AI agent that took what we’d learned about each segment and turned it into personalised, on-brand drafts, so we could write properly for six audiences without six times the hours. Without it, the 148% wouldn’t have been possible at that scale. Alongside it, I used AI workflows to analyse registration and session data faster, and to repurpose content across channels.

But the agent was only as good as what went into it: the conversations, the show data, the voice rules. It hadn’t sat in on a sales call or read this year’s session comments. Use AI to scale the writing, not to decide what to say.

What it did for the results

We wrote separately for marketers, corporate planners, agencies, associations and higher education, hospitality and customers. Email-driven registrations rose 148%. An earlier test in 2024 showed why it was worth the effort:

AudienceEmails sentClick-through rate
Prospects (separate send)52,9953.5%
Customers (separate send)6,8891.6%
Combined list251,9850.8%

And each segment taught us something different:

  • Marketers: subject lines about peer outcomes beat feature-led ones.
  • Corporate prospects: a pain-led subject line, “Burning out? Here’s your turning point”, drove 7 registrations. The question version drove none.
  • Agencies: naming the Partner Summit early in the email worked. Burying it past paragraph two didn’t.
  • Associations and higher education: avoid price in the opening. Mentioning £199 undercut the tone.
  • Customers: “Welcome back” had a 32.6% open rate but only a 3.0% click-through rate, because the body went straight to a discount code. Great first impression, awkward second.
  • Hospitality: naming the specific lunch or session (the CVB lunch, the GM lunch) in the first lines outperformed a general announcement.

How to start

  • Book 30 minutes with whoever owns each vertical or audience, and ask what that group is worried about this year.
  • Pull last year’s session attendance, session feedback and show rate by segment.
  • Write the first line of each email for that segment’s likely question, not the event’s headline.
  • Test one change at a time and keep a note of what each segment responded to.
  • Reuse it next year. Our 2026 copy was built on the previous year’s results for each segment. Your past data is your best copywriter.

Key takeaways

  • Splitting the list is easy. Knowing what each segment cares about is the real work.
  • Talk to vertical owners and the people who speak to each audience every week.
  • Use show data (session attendance, feedback and show rate), not just registration data.
  • Use AI to scale the writing, not to decide what to say.
  • Done properly, segmented messaging grew email-driven registrations by 148% at CONNECT Europe.
